## The post-prescription gap

### 00:27:49 — Aja Beckett

It makes sense, but I think a lot of the institutions that are higher up in the process are, of course, focused on the prescription and the delivery—maybe the titration schedules. It's all well and good to have the label schedule and the clinical trial data. That's very important. But the way people take these medications in the real world is much messier and more complicated. There just isn't time to work one-on-one with patients as they navigate this complicated weekly reality.

What we see as the post-prescription gap is about helping people with those small decisions, not the big ones. We very much want to act in a support role to the patient's relationship with their healthcare team. ### 00:42:11 — Aja Beckett

People who are new to the app tend to convert very quickly. Most of our users purchase a subscription during onboarding and keep it. I think what they are paying for, especially that early on in the process, is clarity, confidence, and continuity.

## Specificity, authenticity, and organic growth

### 00:45:39 — Ashwin Sharma

From my perspective, it’s really insightful that you were previously an app developer and know, through trial and error, that process of getting into the mind of the consumer—and also that you’re a patient.

From a consumer health app perspective, what has been your biggest growth unlock in terms of the channels you explore to acquire new patients?

### 00:45:52 — Aja Beckett

Overall, I think the biggest growth unlock was being specific. We see so many apps in the space that have a history as unrelated apps, or as much more general diet-tracking apps.

With Shotsy, people can immediately understand what it is. It’s literally in the name. Our first icon was a syringe. This is incredibly specific to this unique experience, and users would immediately recommend us to others.

GLP-1 communities are very active online, and I think they can tell immediately when something is built by somebody who really understands the experience.

It’s funny because I’m definitely more of a product person. I’m an iOS engineer; marketing is not my strength. The product had to be very good and resonate with users, because I’m certainly not a genius at engineering viral growth. So I think it was the specificity and the authenticity.

With Reddit in particular, that is a space that really values authenticity. I was clearly a real member of the community. I was posting in one of the subreddits for the medication I was taking for months before it even occurred to me that it would be useful to build an app for it.

There are plenty of probably embarrassing questions that I asked as an honest user trying to understand how to get the most out of the medication I was taking. I think that really comes across, and it’s welcome in a lot of spaces to see somebody who really understands it.

### 00:47:52 — Ashwin Sharma

It speaks volumes about how rapidly it has spread, especially through word of mouth, which is the best marketing you can get.

This is where my naivety comes in. In terms of unit economics, you’ve built a consumer app. How do you think about CAC and LTV? ### 00:48:31 — Aja Beckett

About two-thirds of our new users come from organic sources: word of mouth, people posting, and the fact that we’re well ranked in the iOS App Store and Google Play Store.

In terms of LTV—long-term value, or lifetime value—I think about it more in terms of journey relevance. It’s not just, “How often can we get somebody to open the app?” It’s: “Can we remain useful as their needs change from starting, to titrating, to plateauing, to maintaining, and maybe switching or stopping?”

Retention and lifetime value really come from continuing relevance, not just trying to engineer habit loops.

## Why Shotsy remains independent

### 00:49:03 — Ashwin Sharma

You’ve built this amazing app. Would you ever consider actually selling the prescription?

### 00:49:17 — Aja Beckett

We’ve been asked. We’ve certainly been approached by many who would like to sell prescriptions through us.

There is a lot of value in remaining independent here. This business is built on trust. This is a very personal, very emotional experience for people, and it’s valuable that we can take the role of companion in this journey regardless of where they’re getting their medications, which medication they’re taking, or which delivery mechanism they’re using.

We are agnostic. We exist to support GLP-1 users on their journeys. The fact that we don’t have a business incentive to send them to any particular provider or medication makes this much more valuable for users.

At the end of the day, that makes it a better business for us. This is a consumer subscription app, and the more value we can provide to users, the better we do as a business. I haven’t been tempted. Any partnership like that would have to add obvious value to users. Right now, independence is really very important for our user base.
